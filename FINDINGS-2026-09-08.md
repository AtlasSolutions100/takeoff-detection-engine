# 2026-09-08 — core (excavation) improvements that need no ground truth

Seven changes on `dev`, each with a test that fails on the pre-fix commit.
Nothing here changes a number the earthwork gate pins (see the re-accept
proof at the end); what changes is WHICH rows reach a volume and what the
panel says about them.

## 1. The surface split abstains instead of filing a whole sheet as EXISTING

`earthwork.split_rows_surface` third tier (stroke width) used to put every
row at or below the width median on `existing`. On a sheet with ONE line
weight that is every row - Belle Farms ROAD GRADING (40 rows) and
C2.1-Grading (18 rows) came out all-existing with nothing on the sheet to
say so, which is the "labelled EXIST or skipped" symptom Koby reported.

Now: one distinct weight on the whole sheet -> `surface: null`,
`split_by: "none"`, `split_counts.none`. Two or more weights still split,
against the lower median of the whole sheet (a two-row sheet at 0.2 / 0.9
pt puts the 0.9 on proposed; the old upper median called both existing).
`webui/app.js` honours `"none"` (the row starts on skip) instead of
re-running its own width median over it; the panel shows an "N contours
have no side yet" line with **All existing** / **All proposed** buttons
that stamp every unassigned row as an operator decision; the row tooltip
says NO SIDE YET and why. Corpus measurement (scratchpad
`split_measure.py`, twelve sheets, throwaway store): see the table at the
end of this file. Tests: `tests/test_split_guards.py` (widths section),
`tests/test_surface_adopt.js` (mono case now expects skip).

## 2. The ink rule's polarity comes from the legend when the legend can say

grey = existing / dark = proposed was hard-coded. When the legend names
both contour families and they carry ink (two or more inked rows a side,
`_POLARITY_MIN`), the legend's own polarity is used for the colour-decided
rows: `split_counts.polarity` = `legend-confirmed` | `legend-flipped` |
`convention`, and `ewSplitNote` says which. No corpus sheet flips
(Oncor's legend families are inked the conventional way); the change is
the disclosure plus the guard. Tests: `tests/test_split_guards.py`
(polarity section); `tests/test_ew_suggest.py` asserts carry the key.

## 3. The elevation OCR pass has two clocks and says when it used one

Barn C3.0 (a site plan with no contours) spent the whole 1,400-call
budget on utility and property linework and ran past a 600 s probe timeout
reading nothing, with the panel on "finding elevations" throughout.
`gap_label_elevations(idle_budget_s=, time_budget_s=, diag=)`: a wide
hard cap (`TAKEOFF_OCR_BUDGET_S`, 900 s) and an idle clock
(`TAKEOFF_OCR_IDLE_S`, **off by default**). `diag` (patches planned / OCR
calls / seconds / stopped_early / stop_reason) is cached beside the values
(`p{N}_elevgaps2.diag.json`) and returned as `elevation_ocr`; the panel's
dropped-rows block prints "Label reading stopped early" with the reason.
Test: `tests/test_ocr_budget.py`.

Why the idle clock is off, measured (calls made / first raw read / wall,
offline on this box): Oncor C-04 868 / call 4 / 200 s; Belle ROAD 421 /
call 57 / 96 s; C4 GRADING 517 / call 54 / 105 s; C0.00 288 / call 5 /
55 s; **Barn C3.0 382 / call 158 (2 raw reads, both rejected) / 87 s**;
C2.1 848 / call 5 / **2183 s under load** (2.57 s per call against 0.23
unloaded - another agent's detects on the same box, 10x). So C3.0's
"twenty minutes" was the load, not the sheet, and an idle rule cannot
separate C3.0 (reads at 33 s) from Belle (18 s): under the same 10x load a
120 s idle clock empties Belle first. The first cut of this change (a 300 s
hard cap, no idle rule) ran during the corpus measurement under that load
and emptied C2.1 to 0 rows - the measurement that set the cap at 900 s.
What C3.0 needs is progress on the panel, not a shorter clock (follow-up).

## 4. A collinear surface is refused with a sentence, not a 500

One straight chain a side, or three spot grades along a kerb, passed the
3-sample floor and hit Qhull inside `LinearNDInterpolator` ("initial
simplex is flat"); the route answered a bare 500 Internal Server Error.
`Surface.interpolator` measures collinearity first (`_collinear`, RMS
perpendicular distance under 1e-6 pt) and `_floor_refusal` /
`zone_cut_fill` return `error_code: "surface_collinear"` naming the
surface. Reproduced on C3.0 chains 734/735 before the fix. Test:
`tests/test_ew_collinear.py` (raises QhullError on the pre-fix commit).

## 6. The scale is checked against the dimensions the sheet prints

New `takeoff_engine/dimcheck.py` + `GET /api/job/{id}/page/{p}/scale/dimensions`
(cached per page and scale in force) + a chip in the sheet head. Every
lone dimension string (`60'`, `120'-0"`) is paired with the span it
labels - the bay between the extension ticks either side of it, or the
run it is centred on - and printed feet over paper length is an
independent ft/in reading. Verdicts: agrees / disagrees (the witnesses
agree with EACH OTHER at X, not with the scale) / inconclusive /
no-witnesses / no-scale, with the readings behind it. It never sets the
scale. Measured: Belle Farms Structural p0 (1/8" = 1'-0", 8 ft/in): 7 of 7
lone dimensions read 7.6-7.9 ft/in against their own lines; fed a wrong
4 ft/in the same seven say "disagrees, ~7.9 together". The civil sheets in
the corpus print no lone dimension strings (Barn C3.0's "70' x 9'
PULLTHROUGH STALL" is a sentence, Belle ROAD GRADING's "1140.96'" is an
elevation) and the check says "no witnesses" on them - honest, not
useful, until a sheet prints one. Test: `tests/test_dimcheck.py`
(synthetic sheet built from the four shapes that broke the pairing rule:
one line across three bays, 45° slash ticks, text-mask rectangles whose
edges end on the line, an overall dimension whose bays' extension lines
end on it too). Crops: `out/verify/2026-09-08/dimcheck/`.

## 39. A spot grade is not a contour's height (2026-09-17, dev)

**THE DEFECT, as the owner reported it:** "there are some elevations on
Oncor that are being attached to a contour line from a proposed spot grade
elevation." A spot grade is a POINT the design sets - a wall top, a finish
grade, a rim, an invert - and a contour is a LINE at one height; the sheet
says which it means by printing a tag beside the number ("551.2 BW"). The
nearest-label match read the tag, put it on the chip, and took the number
anyway.

**MEASURED on Oncor C-04** (engine page index 3; scratchpad
`probe_tagged_rows.py`, `probe_c04_labels.py`): 14 of the sheet's rows
carried a tagged spot's height - BW 4, TW 5, EW 3, ME 2 - and FOUR of them
were `inherited`, i.e. one wall spot spread along the contour to further
lines (chains 81, 90, 116, 237). Legend-confirmed contour families were
affected, not just unnamed ones (chain 102, "EXISTING CONTOUR", took 558.5
BW). Independently, 18 of the 89 rows that came from a printed number
carried a FRACTIONAL value on a sheet whose own contour interval is 1 ft.

**THE RULE** (`earthwork.is_built_spot_tag`): a number the sheet tags as a
point on what is being BUILT is refused as a contour seed. It is refused
in the candidate pool, before any pass runs, so the in-break pass cannot
take it, the nearest-label pass falls through to the next number on the
line, and pass 2 cannot spread it. EG and ME - existing ground, match
existing - are NOT refused: they are the existing surface's own points,
and refusing them as well moved C-04's cut by 0 CY, so the narrower rule
is the one taken. The spot grades are not lost either way: they reach the
surfaces as points through `read_spot_grades`, where wall tops stay off
the ground by default and the operator chooses per tag (FINDINGS 36).
Disclosed, never silent: `diag["spot_tag_refused"]` -> the suggest reply's
`spot_tag_refused` -> the panel, beside what the filters dropped.

**WHAT IT DID TO THE NUMBERS** (scratchpad `probe_c04_calc.py`, the real
calc route on the rows the server itself sides, linear surfaces, no limit
of work; reference = the offline analyst's ~2,002 CY cut for C-04):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it was | 163 | 2,279 | +14% | 9,841 |

The row count goes UP (163 -> 170) because a line that no longer takes a

## 41. What backs this number: the volume's own evidence, per job (2026-09-17, dev) — spec stage 3

`CUTFILL-ACCURACY-SPEC.md` §0 settled where the error lives: the
interpolation kernel is within 0.5% of exact on analytic surfaces (0.00%
on a plane) and the grid moves a real sheet's cut by ±0.4% across cell
sizes 1-8 ft, so **a wrong cut/fill is a wrong INPUT**. An estimator
cannot see inputs. A client on a live job put it plainly: "we get no
defensible CY. I won't invent volumes."

So the reply now carries `proof` and the panel prints it - four checks,
each with its own numbers and its own state, and a verdict that is the
worst of them:

| check | what it asks | limit |
|---|---|---|
| spots | does the surface match the elevations the sheet PRINTS as points? | median ≤ 0.25 ft, bias ≤ 0.15 ft |
| model | what do the same rows give under the other surface model? | ≤ 5% |
| grid | what do the same surfaces give on a finer grid? | ≤ 5% |
| region | is the region the estimator's own limit of work, or just where the surfaces overlap? | bounded |

`earthwork.spot_residuals` / `earthwork.volume_proof`; the limits ride with
the answer (`PROOF_LIMITS`) so a reader can disagree with them. GROUND
spots only - ME/EG for the ground now, FG/FIN/FFE/PE/HP/LP/FL for the
ground as designed; a wall top, a rim or a curb is not ground and the
surface is not meant to pass through it. **A check that cannot run reports
`cannot check` and is never counted as a pass.**

**THE DEFECT THE VERIFICATION CAUGHT, and the reason to trust the rest.**
The first live run read the spot residual as **0.00 ft** - because the
panel feeds the sheet's spot grades into the surface as points, so the
check was asking the surface to repeat itself. The same sheet read 0.91 ft
offline where the spots were not inputs. A check that cannot fail is
worthless, so a spot the surface was BUILT from is now **held out**: the
surface is rebuilt without a fifth of them and asked about that fifth,
five times over (standard k-fold), while a spot that is not an input is
compared directly. The result says which ran (`independent` /
`cross-validated`) and every named point carries its own `how`.

**What it measures today** (live, headed - `out/verify/2026-09-17/volume-proof/`):

| sheet | verdict | the checks |
|---|---|---|
| Oncor C-04, unbounded | warn, 1 of 4 | existing surface **0.68 ft** from the sheet's 7 printed ground spots (median, cross-validated), bias +0.61, worst 1.37; other model 27.2% away; grid 0.3% (pass); region unbounded |
| Barn C3.0 | warn, 2 of 4 | **cannot check** - the sheet prints no ground spot, so "the volume rests on the contour lines alone"; other model 11.5%; grid 0.1% (pass) |

The C-04 residual is a real lead, not just a disclosure: a systematic
+0.6 ft bias on the existing surface at the sheet's own match-existing
points is worth ~2,300 CY over that 100,540 SF region, and it is what

## 42. Stage 1 stopped on measurement: a contour-ordering solver cannot be validated on our sheets (2026-09-17, dev)

`CUTFILL-ACCURACY-SPEC.md` stage 1 proposed replacing the one-transect-at-a-
time count with a contour-ORDERING pass: build the adjacency of contour
lines, propagate elevations in interval steps, check consistency. Approved
and started. Four measurements killed it, in order, and each one is worth
keeping so nobody rebuilds it.

**1. They are not fragments.** The first hypothesis was that the unelevated
lines are pieces of labelled contours split at label gaps, so welding would
carry the height along. Pass 2 already welds (endpoints within 60 pt, same
CAD layer, same linetype, tangent continuous) and yields 1 row on Barn
C3.0. Re-run under every relaxation (drop the class, drop the layer,
geometry only, same pen, 120 and 200 pt gaps - scratchpad
`probe_weld.py`): **1 line gained on Barn under all of them**, 25 -> 29 on
C-04, 0 on C2.1, and no welded group ever held two heights. Welding is not
the lever.

**2. What they actually are** (`probe_unelev_geom.py`): on Barn C3.0 the
290 unelevated lines are 118 twins lying on top of another line (17,253
ft), 128 close neighbours (32,179 ft) and 44 separate contours (7,441 ft).
The longest are 400-500 ft members of the EXISTING CONTOUR family sitting
5-6 ft from other UNELEVATED lines - a fan of unlabelled contours with no
label anywhere in it.

**3. The denominator was pessimistic.** Coverage by LENGTH, which is what a
surface is built from: Barn C3.0 **41%** (39,623 of 96,496 ft), C-04 67%.
By line count it reads 17% and 50%. And 142 of Barn's 290 unelevated lines
lie OUTSIDE the limit of work, where they cost the volume nothing. The
honest coverage statement is length, inside the boundary.

**4. THE STOPPER: adjacency does not mean "one interval apart."** Measured
where the answer is known - every adjacency edge whose BOTH ends carry a
printed height (`probe_adjacency_calib.py`, `probe_adjacency_clean.py`):

| sheet | anchor-to-anchor edges | dz = one interval | cleaned (same family + no twins + on-grid anchors) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oncor C-04 | 184 | 33% | **80%** (24 of 30) |
| C2.1 Grading | 35 | 51% | 69% |
| Barn C3.0 | 9 | 22% | **0 edges exist** |

The gap distributions overlap completely (dz 0 edges span 0-44 ft, dz 1
edges 0-56 ft), so geometry cannot separate "the same contour again" from
"the next contour". Cleaning the candidate set helps and is worth keeping
as a rule, but 80% is not a foundation: a solver propagates along chains,
so a one-in-five wrong step compounds, and it would mint heights with the
same confidence as a printed label. On Barn C3.0 - the sheet this was for -
the 17 printed labels produce NO same-family anchor-to-anchor adjacency at
all, so there is nothing to calibrate or propagate along.

**What the sheet actually gives us.** Barn C3.0 prints 17 contour labels
for 350 lines. No inference from 17 anchors can responsibly fill a 350-line
field; the information is not on the drawing. That is a manual-input case
by the spec's own contract, not an algorithm case.

**Stage 1 is therefore rewritten** (spec updated in the same commit):
  a. report coverage by LENGTH inside the limit of work, and give every
     line without a height a stated reason - the half of the original goal
     the evidence fully supports;
  b. keep the cleaned candidate set (same family, twins merged, anchors on
     the family's own interval grid) for the counting that already runs -
     it raises the trustworthy share from 27% to 80% on C-04;
  c. a FAN FILL for the estimator: pick the fan, give one height and a
     direction, the software counts the run and fills it, every value
     flagged operator-counted with the count shown and editable. This is
     what turns a 290-line hole into a few gestures without inventing
     anything.
  d. **stage 2 (breaklines, peak/pit closure) is promoted ahead of it**: it
     is automatic, evidence-backed, and it targets the one systematic error
     we can already measure - the +0.61 ft cross-validated bias on C-04's
     existing surface (FINDINGS 41).

No engine code changed for this entry. The probes are in the scratchpad and
named above.
